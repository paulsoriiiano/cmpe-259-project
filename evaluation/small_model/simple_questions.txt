Question 1: List parks with accessible trails.
Answer:  Malakoff Diggins State Historic Park offers over 25 miles of trails for hiking, some of which are accessible. The self-guided nature trail along Sonoma Creek, located near the campground, is considered accessible according to the park's accessibility information. (Sources: Malakoff Diggins State Historic Park information)

Please note that trail conditions can change, so it's always a good idea to check with the park directly for the most up-to-date information on trail accessibility.

Additionally, Sugarloaf Ridge State Park offers accessible trails, with one mile of paved trail in the park being designated as accessible. (Sources: Sugarloaf Ridge State Park information)

For more information on accessible trails in California State Parks, visitors can contact individual parks or visit the park accessibility information page on the California State Parks website. (Source: California State Parks Accessibility Information)

Question 2: Is Chino Hills pet-friendly?
Answer:  Yes, dogs are allowed only on paved roads and in campgrounds at Chino Hills State Park. They are not permitted on park trails. (Sources: Chino Hills State Park information from <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=648>)

Question 3: Tell me about Red Rock Canyon.
Answer:  Red Rock Canyon State Park is located 25 miles northeast of Mojave on Highway 14. The park is open from sunrise to sunset for day use, while the campground is open 24 hours. Dogs are allowed only in the picnic area, campground, and primitive roads, not on the trails. The park features scenic desert cliffs, buttes, and rock formations at the convergence of the Sierra Nevada and El Paso Ranges. Each tributary canyon has distinctive shapes and vivid colors. The park offers free admission and amenities include hiking, picnic tables, and a visitor center. Nearby attractions include Saddleback Butte State Park, which has a Joshua Tree/creosote habitat, and Red Rock Canyon State Park, known for its colorful cliffs that have been featured in movies and TV shows [1]. During peak season, high demand holiday periods, and weekends, special rates may apply. Be aware that the trails in Red Rock Canyon State Park were impacted by severe weather from Tropical Storm Hilary, so use caution when hiking and bring plenty of drinking water [2].

[2] <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=631> & August 20, 2023 update "Update: Expect a Limited Wildflower Bloom This Spring in Southern California’s State Parks" & August 20, 2023 update "Trails at Red Rock Canyon State Park Impacted by Severe Weather"

Question 5: Which parks can I see bears?
Answer:  Sugarloaf Ridge State Park and Auburn State Recreation Area are two parks where bears, specifically black bears, can be spotted. (Sources: Sugarloaf Ridge State Park information from <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=481> and Auburn State Recreation Area information from <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=502>)

            Additional info:
            Black bears can be seen in both Sugarloaf Ridge State Park and Auburn State Recreation Area. While out for a hike or enjoying the nature, take precautions such as not leaving food unattended, storing food properly, and keeping a safe distance from bears to ensure a pleasant experience for visitors and the bears. (Sources: Sugarloaf Ridge State Park information and Auburn State Recreation Area information)

Question 6: Is there an entry fee for Anza-Borrego?
Answer:  Yes, there is an entry fee for Anza-Borrego Desert State Park. The camping fees include entry for one vehicle and one legally towed vehicle or trailer. Additional vehicles must register and pay additional fees, currently $10.00 each vehicle. [Sources: Anza-Borrego Desert State Park information from <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=638>]

Question 7: Which beaches allow dogs?
Answer:  Dogs are allowed in campgrounds at Monterey State Beach (South of the Monterey Beach Resort hotel), Asilomar State Beach, Carmel River State Beach, and Garrapata State Park. However, dogs are not allowed on the beaches themselves at these locations. At South Carlsbad State Beach and Moss Landing State Beach, dogs are not allowed on the beaches or in campgrounds. New policies are being implemented at some state beaches to protect the western snowy plover, and Salmon Creek Beach and Bodega Dunes Beach are protected areas where dogs, fires, and camping are strictly prohibited. (Sources: <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=660>, <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=574>, <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=451>, <https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=451>)

Question 8: Are there any state parks in San Diego?
Answer:  Yes, there are several state parks in San Diego. Old Town San Diego State Historic Park is located in the heart of San Diego and offers a glimpse into the city's rich history. San Clemente State Beach and South Carlsbad State Beach are both popular destinations for swimming, surfing, and camping. China Camp State Park, located near the San Pablo Bay, offers beautiful views of the waterfront and protects endangered species like the California clapper rail and the salt marsh harvest mouse. For more specific details, contact the relevant park offices or visit the California State Parks website.
